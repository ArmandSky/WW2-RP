Netherlands Falls in 25 Days

Amsterdam, Netherlands 1st of December, 1937

In a rapid and devastating campaign, the German Wehrmacht has overrun the Netherlands in just twenty-five days. The German offensive began with a ruthless air assault that quickly shattered the small but resolute Dutch Air Force. Within days, the skies above the Low Countries were dominated by the Luftwaffe.

Dutch defenses crumble under relentless assault

After fifteen days of desperate fighting, Dutch homeland units were reduced to battered remnants clinging to the last defensive lines around Utrecht and Rotterdam. German infantry and armor pressed forward relentlessly, isolating pockets of defenders and forcing their surrender. When Germany demanded capitulation, the remaining Dutch soldiers chose defiance. In response, the Wehrmacht unleashed its final blow, wiping out the surviving Dutch field army and leaving no organized military force standing.

Monarchy spared as conquest completes

By the twenty-fifth day, the entire country was under German control. The German command, however, granted the Dutch royal family safe passage and gave them seven days to depart the Netherlands unharmed. This rare clemency came as the last symbolic vestige of the old Dutch order vanished from the homeland.

Colonial forces surrender after homeland falls

The Dutch East Indies did not fare any better. The German Second Fleet stood offshore, waiting to strike until the homeland was subdued. The Royal Netherlands Navy’s main remaining units—centered on a light cruiser and two destroyers in Sumatra—held guard for a time. After Amsterdam fell, Berlin broadcast a final demand for surrender to the colonial forces. With news of the homeland’s fall, most local garrisons capitulated without further resistance.

A handful of ships tried to flee. After failing to comply with a second surrender broadcast, the German battleships Ludendorff and Gneisenau together with the heavy cruisers Schleswig-Holstein, Schlesen, and Hannover intercepted the fleeing light cruiser and destroyers. They were sunk within minutes, and survivors were detained.

War!! The Sleeping Giant vs The Rising Sun & The Lady of the Seas

Washington D.C., United States of America 10th of November, 1937

So far the war between the United States and Great Britain & Japan has been rather cold, neither side brazenly attacking the other, like two heavyweights sizing each other up to see who will flinch first. The USA has not yet invaded the half of Canada still garrisoned by British troops, as it is the middle of winter and neither side would fare well in those snowy, forested, and often mountainous conditions. Our war correspondents believe the US will wait out the heavy weather before invading.

Naval maneuvering continues

Meanwhile at sea both sides are maneuvering their fleets in a most classified way, trying to outwit the other.

Suriname Captured

Paramaribo, Suriname 4th of December, 1937

Greek invasion forces had been poised to strike the Dutch colony for days, but the first sign of resistance came from a single Dutch sloop defending the harbor at Paramaribo. The lone vessel stood between the approaching fleet and the colony’s remaining defenders.

Ultimatum delivered

Greece, backed by a German heavy cruiser, issued an ultimatum to the sloop and the Dutch garrison: surrender and be spared, or face annihilation. Facing overwhelming odds, the Dutch defenders accepted the terms. They were promised that no harm would come to them and that they would receive safe passage back to the Netherlands.

Safe passage turns tragic

The surrender was honored. The Dutch garrison was evacuated under the agreed conditions and loaded onto a convoy ship bound for the Netherlands. But just before the convoy reached the German blockade line, disaster struck. The transport caught fire in what most observers believe was a freak accident.

The exact cause remains unclear. Most experts suspect poor maintenance aboard the convoy ship allowed a spark to ignite stores or fuel, producing a blaze that could not be contained. The fire broke out before the convoy reached German-controlled waters, turning a negotiated surrender into a grim maritime tragedy.

With the fall of Suriname complete, Greece has cemented its control over the Dutch colony, and the once-proud harbor defense is now a cautionary tale of fragile surrender terms and the dangers of war at sea.

Brazilian Overlords in South America

Caracas, Venezuela 8th of December, 1937

After several months of infighting and civil war, rebel forces in Venezuela and Bolivia have overthrown their governments. The uprisings were backed by Brazil, which the rebels now cite as their protector and sponsor.

New order under Brazilian oversight

The rebel leaders have pledged their nations to Brazil. They will govern locally under a system of Brazilian overlordship and, by all intents and purposes, will be part of the Brazilian government.

This new arrangement is being presented as a better alternative to the dictatorial regimes that were toppled. Brazil’s influence now stretches deeper into northern and central South America as the region enters a new phase of political realignment.
